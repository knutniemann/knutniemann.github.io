Political Distance and International Trade

Greater political distance reduces trade: an average within-pair increase lowers trade by 3.1 percent—about half the effect of removing a regional trade agreement.

Using more than 50 years of bilateral and domestic trade among 166 countries, I introduce political distance based on UN General Assembly votes as a trade cost in a structural gravity model. The effect is strongest during heightened geopolitical tension and for simple-to-produce consumer goods, and weaker among liberal democracies.

How US Interstate Migration Responds to Political Shocks

with Jonas Adolph

We study county-level migration after toss-up gubernatorial elections. Post-election emigration is five percent higher in counties that supported the losing candidate, driven mainly by increased out-migration from Democratic counties after Republican wins.

Firm Expectations and News: Micro v Macro

with Benjamin Born, Zeno Enders, Manuel Menkhoff, and Gernot J. Müller

German and Italian firm surveys show that expectations overreact to firm-specific news but underreact to aggregate news. An “island illusion” model rationalizes the pattern and its aggregate implications.

About

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Chair of International Economics and European Integration, led by Anna Gumpert, at the University of Tübingen. My fields are international economics, applied macroeconomics, expectation formation, and political economy.

I received my PhD in Economics from the University of Tübingen's School of Business and Economics in 2023. In 2026, I was a visiting researcher in the Research Unit at Danmarks Nationalbank.
